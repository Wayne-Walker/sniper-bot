### Added
- **V-bottom confirmation for the weekly structure turn — `macro_bottom.py` (2026-09-25).** `_structure_turn()` compared the last two weekly swing lows and required a **higher low**, which a low that is never retested can never produce: the cycle low *is* the most recent swing low and it is lower than the one before it, so the test returns `False` indefinitely. That is a **permanent** miss, strictly worse than a late one. BTC 2026 was the case — low $57,800 (2026-06-29), +45% off it with the 20W MA reclaimed, and the weekly test still read `False`. New `turn_kind()` returns `higher_low` | `v_bottom` | `None` and confirms a V on time + rally instead: the lowest low of the last `V_TURN_LOOKBACK` (26w), at least `V_TURN_WEEKS` (4) behind us, price `V_TURN_RALLY` (20%) off it, 20W reclaimed — the reclaim is required on both paths, so neither fires on a low that is still falling. **Walked forward week by week**, `v_bottom` confirms BTC's 2026 low on **2026-08-17** (old test: never) and *also improves the 2022 bottom* it already caught — **2023-01-23 at $23,742** vs the higher-low test's 2023-03-27 at $28,172, nine weeks and 16% of price earlier, with `higher_low` still confirming afterwards, so it is a strict improvement rather than a trade. Honest limit, verified not fixed: the April–May 2026 bear rally printed a textbook higher low above the 20W MA while the real low was two months out, and no low-anchoring filter separates that from a true turn in real time (the prior low genuinely *was* the lowest in 26 weeks at the time) — which is why the turn stays worth only 15 of 100 points. The turn shape is surfaced on the card's factor line, in the alert's "what this means", in the scanner log and in Firebase as `turn_kind`. The 🟢 structure alert is now **suppressed while the phase is `recovery`**, where a V confirms on nearly the same conditions as the 🌱 recovery alert and one event would otherwise ping twice — without it the first run after deploy would have fired four at once (BTC/ETH/XRP/BNB all flip `False`→`v_bottom`). Verified end-to-end with Telegram and Firebase stubbed: deploy run 0 alerts, re-run 0 alerts. Scores move but no coin changes zone (all five remain `wait`): BTC 19.9→15.0, ETH 20.5→23.5, SOL 38.0→27.1 (`higher_low`), XRP 28.0→20.0, BNB 5.0→20.0. (`macro_bottom.py`, `README.md`)
- **Cycle phase — direction for an unsigned score, `macro_bottom.py` (2026-09-25).** Reviewing BTC's macro card showed the scanner reading `0/100 wait` while the cycle low was **12 weeks behind it** — and a cycle top would print exactly the same card. Root cause is structural, not a threshold: every factor in the score (below-200W, washed-out RSI, fear, negative funding) measures distress happening **now**, and distress ends at the low, so the score is guaranteed to collapse in the week price turns (BTC went **48.4 → 5.0** in the single breakout week of 2026-08-17) and is silent exactly when its information is most valuable. New `cycle_phase()` supplies the missing direction from the candles already fetched — **no extra request**: it locates the cycle high over `CYCLE_LOOKBACK` (156w), the lowest low since it, the rally off that low and the 20W reclaim, and returns one of **markup · markdown · capitulation · basing · recovery** (`MIN_BEAR_DD` 25%, `RECOVERY_RALLY` 20%, `CONFIRM_WEEKS` 4, `MARKUP_NEAR_HIGH` 10%). That answers "was this cycle ever actually washed out?" — **BTC peaked at ≥56 and never reached the buy zone** (needs 60), while ETH and SOL both touched 60. New 🌱 *MACRO CYCLE PHASE — RECOVERY* alert fires on entering `recovery` only; the other transitions are gradual and update state silently. Dedup follows the existing pattern via a new `phase` key in `reports/macro_state.json`, with an explicit guard that a row written **before** this feature (no `phase` key) seeds silently instead of counting as a transition — verified end-to-end with Telegram and Firebase stubbed: deploy run 0 alerts + state seeded, re-run 0 alerts, forced `basing`→`recovery` exactly 1 alert. ### Changed
- **Percentile-normalised scoring investigated and rejected — no code change (2026-09-25).** Recorded so it is not re-proposed. The hypothesis was that the funding and 200W factors use absolute thresholds calibrated on the 2018/2022 bears (funding ≤ −20%/yr; price ≤ −20% below the 200W MA) that modern market structure no longer reaches, and that scoring each against its *own* trailing distribution would restore the signal — BTC's 2026 bottom peaked at ≈56 and never crossed the 60 accumulate line. **Measured on both, and the data refutes it.** (1) *Funding*: at the 2026-06-29 cycle low BTC's trailing-7d mean funding was **+6.9%/yr — the 83rd percentile** of its own history (ETH 55th, SOL 79th). Funding was *high* in its own terms at the bottom, not low, so no calibration recovers a signal that is not there; the Binance `fundingRate` endpoint also caps around 1,500 records (~16 months), too short to span a cycle and be a meaningful percentile base. (2) *200W ratio*: percentile pays **less** than the absolute rule at every bottom measured — BTC 7.7 vs 12.6/30, ETH 26.8 vs 30.0, SOL 25.6 vs 30.0 — because the ratio distribution is bull-skewed (BTC median 1.57, max 3.78), so merely sitting *at* the 200W MA is only the ~18th percentile of a coin's own history. Conclusion: the score was not miscalibrated. BTC 2026 was genuinely shallow **in 200W terms** (ratio bottomed at 1.016 against 0.68 in 2022) and `watch` was the correct read; the real defects were the missing direction (fixed by the cycle phase) and the structure turn's permanent miss (fixed above). One real, separate issue stands unaddressed: funding contributed 0 for the entire bear by both calibrations, so 20 of the 100 points were unreachable and every score was effectively out of 80 — surfacing that ceiling on the card needs ~3 paged funding requests per coin and has not been built.
